Chinese hot-rolled steel imports to South Korea fell 77% after anti-dumping duties. Here's where the buying went

Imports of Chinese hot-rolled steel into South Korea fell 77% in January–October 2026 after anti-dumping duties. Japan now supplies 65% of Korea's hot-rolled imports, up from 44%; Taiwan and Vietnam grew several-fold; and Chinese products outside the duties, cold-rolled coil and semi-finished steel such as slab, rose 58% and 88%.

Written by AINov 17, 2026
Report an error

01A new map for Korea's hot-rolled imports

If you sell steel into South Korea, the question since Seoul started imposing anti-dumping duties is simple: where did the orders go? Korea Customs Service import data for January–October 2026 give a clear answer for hot-rolled steel, the coil that feeds car parts, construction and pipe mills. The figures below leave out heavy plate.

Imports from China fell 76.7%, from 1,215,000 tonnes to 283,000 tonnes. Imports from Japan rose 13.2%, from 1,004,000 to 1,137,000 tonnes. Taiwan went from 51,000 to 187,000 tonnes, 3.7 times as much, and Vietnam from 15,000 to 123,000 tonnes, 8.1 times as much. Total hot-rolled imports fell 23.4%, from 2.29 million to 1.75 million tonnes. These figures cover HS 7208 only. We also checked the alloy-steel hot-rolled codes the duty covers (under HS 7225.30, 7225.40, 7226.20 and 7226.91): imports from China fell 72.9%, from 69,000 to 19,000 tonnes, so Chinese shipments did not shift there.

In short, while Chinese imports fell by 932,000 tonnes, imports from Japan, Taiwan and Vietnam together rose by about 377,000 tonnes, and total imports fell by about 536,000 tonnes. Changes in Korean demand could also play a part; the trade data cannot separate the two. China's share of Korea's hot-rolled imports dropped from 53.1% to 16.1%; Japan's rose from 43.8% to 64.8%.

02Same duties, different timing

In Korea, the Korea Trade Commission investigates dumping and recommends duties, and the finance ministry imposes them by ministerial ordinance. The rates for the two countries are close. Provisional duties started on Sept. 23, 2025, and were extended to nine months, through June 22, 2026; the next day an ordinance made them definitive for five years on June 23, 2026: 31.58% to 33.43% for Japanese suppliers and 28.16% to 33.10% for Chinese ones.

Both countries' biggest mills can avoid the duty. The same ordinance exempts suppliers that agreed to sell at or above a minimum price, known as price undertakings. On the Japanese side that covers JFE Steel, Nippon Steel and Tokyo Steel and the trading houses that export their coil, including Korean-owned traders such as SeAH Japan and POSCO International Japan. On the Chinese side it covers big mills including Baoshan Iron & Steel, Angang, Shougang and Shagang, shipping through designated exporters. The floor is reset every quarter based on Chinese and Japanese domestic prices and the won-dollar exchange rate. The undertakings took effect with the definitive duties on June 23, 2026.

Splitting the year at that date tells a sharper story. In January–May, before the definitive duties and the undertakings took effect, Japanese hot-rolled imports slipped 4.4%, from 588,000 to 562,000 tonnes, and Chinese imports were just 15,000 tonnes, down from 740,000. Taiwan (from 1,000 to 105,000 tonnes) and Vietnam (from 2,000 to 58,000 tonnes) grew fastest but replaced only a fraction. From June to October (the undertakings applied from June 23), Japanese imports jumped 38.1%, from 417,000 to 575,000 tonnes, and 268,000 tonnes came in from China, against 475,000 a year earlier. The timing lines up, but customs data do not show which shipments entered under an undertaking, so they cannot prove the exemptions caused the rebound.

Prices swapped places. Japanese hot-rolled coil averaged $531 a tonne, up 2.4% from $519; Chinese coil rose 15.8%, from $489 to $566. A year ago Chinese coil landed about $30 a tonne below Japanese; in January–October 2026 it averaged about $35 above it. Average unit values also move when the product mix changes, so treat them as a guide rather than a quote.

03Cold-rolled coil and slab, outside the duties

Chinese products not covered by anti-dumping duties grew. Cold-rolled coil, hot-rolled coil rolled thinner again at room temperature, rose 58.1% from 389,000 to 615,000 tonnes; China supplies 95.5% of Korea's cold-rolled imports, although Korea's total cold-rolled imports also rose 63.4%. Semi-finished steel such as slab and billet from China rose 88.4%, from 318,000 to 599,000 tonnes, but Japanese semis fell 14.1%, from 706,000 to 606,000 tonnes, so Korea's total semi-finished imports grew only 7.9%. Slab is the feedstock rolling mills turn into hot-rolled coil and plate; the data do not show who bought it or what it became.

A year earlier Chinese cold-rolled coil cost $585 a tonne, 20% more than Chinese hot-rolled at $489. In January–October 2026 it averaged $567, almost exactly the $566 for hot-rolled, mostly because Chinese hot-rolled got dearer. Against the average for all imported hot-rolled coil, which rose from $504 to $538, the cold-rolled premium narrowed from 16% to 5%.

Zinc-coated steel took a different path. In April 2026 the Trade Commission recommended provisional duties of 22.34% to 33.67% on Chinese galvanized cold-rolled steel thinner than 4.75 mm. Imports of Chinese coated flat steel fell from a monthly average of 159,000 tonnes in January–May to 14,000 tonnes in July, then climbed back to 52,000 tonnes in September and 42,000 in October. For January–October as a whole they fell 30.3%, from 1.41 million to 980,000 tonnes. This customs category mixes covered and uncovered products, so the data alone cannot say which kind came back. Vietnamese coated flat steel rose from 326 tonnes to 26,760 tonnes, still small next to China.

04What could be blocked next

On Oct. 23 the Trade Commission recommended five-year duties of 22.34% to 33.67% on Chinese galvanized cold-rolled steel, with price undertakings for 10 exporters. The finance minister has yet to decide. On Sept. 17 it recommended provisional duties of 25.08% to 27.96% on Chinese steel bar, and on Oct. 8 it opened a case on Chinese seamless pipe, with a preliminary ruling due in February 2027.

Korea has also widened its anti-circumvention rules. The enforcement decree of the Customs Act, the presidential decree that sets out how the law is applied, was amended on Nov. 28, 2025, to treat changing the shape, form or use of a dutied product without changing its essential character as circumvention, even if the tariff code changes. Simple assembly or processing in a third country of materials from the dutied supplier country also counts. If the Trade Commission finds circumvention, the same duty can be extended to those goods. The trade data do not show whether any of the cold-rolled or slab imports would meet that test; the decree's list does not cover processing inside Korea, such as rolling imported slab in a Korean mill.

For suppliers, access to the Korean market now turns less on the headline duty rate than on price undertakings and where the product lines are drawn. For Korean mills, imported hot-rolled coil has become scarcer and Chinese coil dearer since the duties took effect, while duty-free cold-rolled and slab imports grew over the same period. The country-by-product figures here can be updated when final November data come out in mid-December; the Nov. 1–20 preliminary figures due at 9 a.m. Seoul time on Nov. 23 (7 p.m. Nov. 22 in New York) do not break out countries or products.
