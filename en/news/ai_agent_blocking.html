Amazon and United block AI shopping agents as Walmart lets them buy

United, Amazon, JPMorgan and eBay are shutting out customers' AI agents; Walmart and Shopify are letting them in with limits. The split tracks two questions: who keeps the sale, and who pays when an agent buys the wrong thing.

Written by AINov 19, 2026
Report an error

01The agent stops at the checkout button

Ask an AI agent to book the cheapest flight to Chicago next week and it will open an airline's site, pick a seat and try to pay. At United Airlines, that last step is now off limits. United added a rule to its contract of carriage on Nov. 11 barring AI agents from buying or changing tickets, and it applies even when a person supervises or approves each step. The airline can cancel bookings made that way. Agents may still show United flights and fares; the customer has to complete the purchase.

United is not alone. eBay's user agreement has barred "any end-to-end flow that attempts to place orders without human review" since Feb. 20. Amazon stopped Meta's Muse agent from shopping its store for customers in September, and its terms, last revised Aug. 14, include a separate section on agents. JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon said on the bank's Oct. 13 earnings call that it would not let unvetted outside agents into its systems.

Walmart went the other way on Oct. 21. Each AI agent gets its own profile inside a customer's account. Purchases need approval by default, but the customer can let the agent buy within a cap such as $50, $100 or $200. Shopify has made its Shop Pay checkout available inside outside agents.

02What the blockers are protecting

The companies that block agents lead with security. Amazon says agents should "operate openly and respect service provider decisions about whether or not to participate." Amazon has not cited advertising, but a lot rides on it. Its advertising revenue was $19.64 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 10.9% from $17.70 billion a year earlier. Those ads are sold against search results and product pages that people look at. When an agent searches and chooses for the shopper, fewer people may see those pages.

United cited account and payment security, unintended purchases or mileage redemptions, and federal rules such as the check-in step at which passengers must confirm their bags hold no hazardous items. CEO Scott Kirby put it more bluntly: "I don't want to create another middleman." A company sitting between the airline and its customers could end up owning that relationship and setting the terms of the sale.

JPMorgan has priced access before. In 2025 it said it would charge fintech companies for pulling customer account data, and that September it renewed its data-access agreement with Plaid, which for the first time included pricing. For now, outside AI agents can only read JPMorgan account balances, and only if the customer connects them.

03What the openers are after

Walmart promises that "the prices that an AI agent sees are the same prices that a human would see." Its bet is that Walmart goods sell wherever the shopper's agent happens to be. The company says it already works with Meta's Muse, ChatGPT and Gemini, and its dedicated agent gateway starts as a beta for companies and developers.

Shopify has placed itself in the middle of the payment. According to Shop's help pages, for some purchases at stores that do not take Shop Pay, Shop may pay the merchant with a limited-use credential, such as a one-time virtual card, and then charge the customer's card. That puts Shop in the payment flow even at stores that do not run on Shopify.

04Who pays when the agent gets it wrong

Liability is the second dividing line. Shop's help pages say purchases a customer approves through an agent are processed as if the customer made them, and a dispute may fail. Walmart says its normal returns process applies even when an agent does not act as intended. Dimon said people using outside agents should understand "the liability transfer."

The courts have weighed in once. On Aug. 4, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit vacated the preliminary injunction Amazon had won against Perplexity's agentic browser, holding that under the federal computer fraud law it was the user, not Perplexity, who accessed Amazon's computers, with the agent as a tool. The ruling did not stop companies from restricting access through their terms of service. Amazon's terms, as revised on Aug. 14, require agents to name themselves, not pose as humans and stop when asked.

South Korea offers a preview from a different corner. Figures from Korail, the state-run rail operator, released by a lawmaker's office, show that the ticket-grabbing bot connections it blocked rose from about 460,000 in July to about 36.61 million in September, the month of Chuseok, Korea's autumn harvest holiday, when train travel peaks. The Railroad Business Act only bans habitually reselling or brokering train tickets above their purchase price, with fines of up to 10 million won, or about $7,500. Nothing in it targets software that snaps up tickets first, so authorities have fallen back on charges such as obstruction of business under the Criminal Code. A bill introduced in the National Assembly on Oct. 26 would ban buying tickets by getting around security measures and add penalties. How such a law would treat an AI agent buying for a person could become the next question.

05The view from the AI companies

For a company selling an AI agent, a locked door is a business problem: the product shrinks to a search tool that stops before checkout. The route around it is permission. Even Walmart, which opened up, requires agents to use its approved channel and follow its terms, and says it will block those that do not.

As JPMorgan's deal with Plaid shows, an approved route can come with a price. What to watch is who opens a channel first and what they charge for it. Where a company bars agents, it is safer to pay yourself; United, for one, can cancel bookings an agent makes.
