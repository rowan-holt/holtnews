For the first time, more than half of Korean employees earn 3 million won or more a month

The share earning 5 million won or more also hit a first-half record, but the gap between industries was wide.

HOLT NEWS
Oct 28, 2026

01What happened

In a survey released on October 27, Statistics Korea found that 51.9% of wage earners made 3 million won or more a month. It is the first time the share passed half since the survey began in 2013.

The survey reflects April of this year and covers 22.37 million employees. Pay means the monthly average from the main job over the past three months, including bonuses and pay in kind.

02How pay was spread

22.8% earned between 3 and 4 million won, 11.5% between 4 and 5 million won, and 17.6% earned 5 million won or more.

The 5-million-plus share rose 1.2 percentage points from a year earlier, the highest first-half figure on record. Still, 48.1% earned less than 3 million won, and the 2-to-3 million won band, at 29%, remains the largest group.

03Why pay rose

Rising wages and prices played a big part. Large bonuses in the first half, mainly at chip companies, also pushed up the share of high earners.

04Big gaps between industries

In finance and insurance, 40.5% earned 5 million won or more, the highest of any industry. Professional, scientific and technical services followed at 37.5%, and information and communications at 35.8%. In manufacturing, the share rose from 22.9% to 25.4%.

In health and social welfare, only 5.8% did. That sector is also growing fast. Jobs at non-residential welfare facilities, including home care, rose by more than 150,000 in a year to 1.84 million.

05What it means

More people earn more, but prices rose too, so many may not feel much better off.

Because much of the rise came from bonuses, it could reverse if the chip boom slows. Low pay in care work, where jobs are growing fastest, remains a problem to solve.
