Charging Koreans more than buyers abroad could bring penalties under a new competition rule

Dominant firms that charge more in Korea without good reason could be found to be abusing their power.

HOLT NEWS
Nov 7, 2026

01What happened

On November 6, Korea's Fair Trade Commission announced a draft change to the rules under its competition law. It changes how the agency judges unfair pricing by companies that dominate a market. This is the first change to that standard since 2001.

The draft is open for comments until December 16. The commission plans to finish the change in the first half of 2027.

02What changes

Until now, a price had to be "markedly" higher than cost to be a problem. The draft lowers that bar to "substantially" higher.

It also widens the comparison. A price that is substantially above cost, and also substantially above the usual price of the same or similar product in other markets, could count as abuse if there is no good reason for it. Other markets include markets abroad. The commission's example: a subscription that costs 10,000 won a month in the US and elsewhere, but 20,000 won in Korea.

Keeping prices high is covered too. That means raising prices quickly when costs rise, but not lowering them when costs fall. The commission has punished this kind of behavior in price-fixing cases involving several companies, but never as an abuse by one dominant firm.

03Are penalties automatic?

No. The commission must first decide whether a company dominates its market, looking at market share, how hard it is for new firms to enter, and the size of competitors.

Then it checks whether there is a good reason for the higher price in Korea. The commission says the change does not target any particular company or product.

04Criticism

Critics say prices can differ between countries because of exchange rates, taxes, tariffs, shipping and labor costs. Others say "substantially" is vague, which makes it hard for companies to know where the line is.

05What it could mean

Consumers would gain grounds to challenge subscription fees and service prices that are higher than abroad. Dominant firms would need to be ready to explain why Korean prices differ.

The real impact will depend on how "substantially" is read and whether a first case is brought. The wording could also become more specific after the comment period.
