Naver–Dunamu merger: Korea's antitrust staff flag Upbit and a won stablecoin that doesn't exist yet

Staff at Korea's Fair Trade Commission say combining the owner of Naver Pay with the owner of Upbit, the country's biggest crypto exchange, could substantially restrict competition, from mobile payments to a won stablecoin market that does not exist yet. They are recommending conditions rather than a ban, and the deal is already nine months behind schedule.

Nov 10, 2026

01When a crypto exchange joins a payment app

What happens when a country's biggest crypto exchange joins one of its biggest payment apps? South Korea is about to find out. On November 10, the Korea Fair Trade Commission (KFTC), the country's competition watchdog, said its full commission would now take up a deal between Naver Financial, which runs Naver Pay, and Dunamu, which runs Upbit, Korea's biggest crypto exchange. Naver Financial is a subsidiary of Naver, the company behind Korea's top search portal. The KFTC's staff began reviewing the deal when it was filed in November 2025.

The deal is an all-stock swap. Naver Financial would acquire all of Dunamu's shares, making Dunamu a wholly owned subsidiary, and Dunamu shareholders would get about 2.54 Naver Financial shares for each share they hold. According to Naver's filing on November 26, 2025, an outside valuation put Dunamu at about 3.06 times the value of Naver Financial. Naver Financial would issue new shares worth about 15.13 trillion won, roughly $11.2 billion, to Dunamu's shareholders.

The commission's investigating staff concluded that the deal could substantially restrict competition and sent their report to both companies on November 6. The final decision rests with the full commission, the KFTC's top decision-making body of nine commissioners, which is not bound by the staff's report.

02Why 10 markets for two companies that don't compete?

Naver Pay and Upbit do different things, yet the staff looked at 10 markets: mobile payments, crypto exchanges, search portals, online advertising, online shopping, financial data-sharing services, trading in unlisted shares, platforms for investing in listed stocks, online secondhand marketplaces, and the issuance of stablecoins pegged to the Korean won.

Put simply, the staff counted every point where a Naver service meets a Dunamu service. They found 12 overlaps. Only two are horizontal, meaning the companies already compete head to head: stock-investing platforms and secondhand marketplaces. Both fell into a "safe harbor": market concentration is low or barely rises after the deal, so harm to competition is presumed unlikely.

The concern is the conglomerate links: ties between businesses that neither compete nor sit in the same supply chain. In six of them, the crypto exchange market would be tied to another market: payments, search, advertising, shopping, financial data or unlisted-share trading. The staff stressed that the deal would bring together market leaders, such as Korea's top mobile payment service and its top crypto exchange.

03A market that doesn't exist yet

The 10th market has not been created. A won stablecoin is a crypto token designed to hold its value at one Korean won. Korea has not yet passed a law that says who may issue one. Korean media report that a government bill is due this month.

Both companies are expected to enter that business, so the staff found the remaining four overlaps around stablecoins. Issuing a stablecoin and running an exchange, and running an exchange and a payment app, are vertical links, steps in the same chain. Issuing a stablecoin and running a payment app or a shopping site are conglomerate links.

Here's why that matters: one company could end up minting the coin, listing it for trading, and letting you pay with it at checkout. The regulator put that chain under review before the market even exists.

04What could you lose?

The KFTC has not said exactly which behavior worries it. Its merger review guidelines look at three risks in conglomerate deals: whether a likely future rival disappears, whether existing rivals get pushed out, and whether it becomes harder for anyone new to enter.

For users, it could look like this: Naver search, Naver shopping and the Naver Pay checkout screen could steer you toward Upbit or a Naver-linked stablecoin. Other exchanges and payment companies would then have fewer ways to reach customers. With fewer places to choose from, the biggest company has more room to set fees and terms.

05Conditions, not a ban

This hearing is less about whether to block the deal and more about which conditions to attach. After the staff told the companies of their preliminary view, they submitted proposed remedies. The staff consulted outside experts, asked for changes, and the companies sent a final version.

That process exists because of a 2024 change to Korea's competition law, in effect since August 7, 2024, that lets merging companies offer their own fixes. If the companies accept a staff report that includes the revised remedies, the commission can shorten both the hearing and the time it takes to write its decision. The staff's recommended conditions are a mix of things the companies must do and must not do. The details have not been made public.

The commissioners can change or add conditions. The deal was filed on November 28, 2025, so it has taken almost a year to reach a hearing.

06What's left before the deal closes

The KFTC says it will hold the hearing soon. After that, the deal still needs government approvals, including approval for a change in Naver Financial's major shareholder, which Korea's credit information law requires, according to Naver's filing. Korean media also report that the government's planned digital asset law may cap any single shareholder's stake in a crypto exchange at 15% to 20%. That matters here because the deal would make Naver Financial the sole owner of Upbit's operator.

The timetable has already slipped. The first filing set the share swap for June 30, 2026. Amended filings in March, July and October 2026 each pushed it back three months. The latest, filed on October 7, 2026, set the shareholder meetings for February 26, 2027 and the swap for March 31, 2027, nine months later than first planned.

Shareholders can still derail it. If shareholders who oppose the deal at either company demand that it buy back their shares for 1.2 trillion won (about $890 million) or more, the contract can be terminated. The companies can change that threshold by agreement before calling the shareholder meetings. If shareholders at either company vote the deal down, it falls through automatically.
