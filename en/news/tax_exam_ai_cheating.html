Korea's top tax exam scorer admits AI cheating. Is it a crime?

The person who topped South Korea's 2026 certified tax accountant exam has turned themselves in to police, saying they fed the questions to generative AI through a hidden camera. Losing the pass is one thing. The bigger question is whether cheating on a licensing exam is a crime. The tax accountant law has no penalty for it, so any charge would come from the Criminal Act, where the likely offenses carry up to five years in prison.

Written by AINov 15, 2026
Report an error

01What is known, and what isn't

On the morning of November 15, the person who placed first on South Korea's 63rd certified tax accountant exam went on a lawyer's YouTube channel and admitted to cheating on both rounds of this year's test. The candidate, identified only as A, said they photographed the questions with a tiny camera, sent them to a generative AI tool and read the answers off a smartphone. In Korea, certified tax accountants are licensed professionals who prepare tax filings and represent taxpayers before the tax authorities, a profession separate from CPAs. That same day A handed a written confession to the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency, which assigned the case to its major crimes unit.

Suspicion started with the scores. A missed just one of 160 multiple-choice questions in the first round, according to Korean media, with perfect marks in three of four subjects.

Much is still unverified. The method described so far comes only from A. Whether other test-takers did the same, or whether anyone helped, is for the police to establish.

02Can cheating land A in court?

Voiding the exam and a five-year ban, both set out in the tax accountant law, are administrative penalties. Criminal liability is a separate question, and the tax accountant law does not answer it: its penalty clauses cover things like practicing without a license or leaking clients' secrets, not cheating on the exam. Any criminal charge would have to come from the Criminal Act.

Korean media have pointed to two offenses. Article 137 punishes anyone who obstructs a public official's duties by deception, with up to five years in prison or a fine of up to 10 million won (about $7,500). Article 314 punishes obstructing another person's business by deception or force, with up to five years or a fine of up to 15 million won (about $11,200). Korea's Supreme Court has held that obstructing a public official's duties falls under Article 137, not Article 314. The tax exam is held under the finance minister's authority but administered by HRD Korea, a public agency under the labor ministry whose staff are not civil servants. Which charge fits will depend on whose work was disrupted, something the investigation and any trial will have to settle.

A better score obtained unfairly is not automatically a crime. In 1966 the Supreme Court upheld the acquittal of a student who had memorized answers from a copy of the marking scheme handed over by a sister. The student had not obtained the sheet by any wrongful act of their own, the court reasoned, and a candidate who happened to know the answers could not be expected to leave them off the page. What A describes, smuggling in a camera and sending the questions out, is a different situation. Whether it happened that way is for the police to establish.

Turning oneself in counts for something. Article 52 of the Criminal Act lets a court reduce or waive the sentence of someone who turns themselves in to investigators, though it is not obliged to, and whether A's statement to the police counts as surrender is also for the court to decide. And whatever happens in a criminal court, the exam can still be voided and the five-year ban imposed under the tax accountant law.

03The law says 'stop' or 'void', not 'revoke'

Cheating on the exam is covered by Article 5-3 of the Certified Tax Accountant Act. It says the minister of finance and economy shall stop or void the exam of anyone who cheats, and shall bar that person from the exam for five years from the date of the decision. The ban is mandatory, not discretionary. Under the act's enforcement decree, that power is delegated to the commissioner of the National Tax Service.

Set that beside Korea's other licensing laws and one difference stands out. Article 5-2 of the Certified Public Accountant Act requires the Financial Services Commission, the financial regulator, to stop the exam or revoke the decision to pass. Article 17 of the Bar Examination Act gives the justice minister the same two options, though its exam ban is discretionary. The tax accountant law does not.

The article says the exam shall be voided, and it does not limit that to cheating caught on the day. What it does not spell out is what happens to a pass that has already been announced once the exam is void. Korean media expect a process to cancel the pass to follow.

The general law also offers a route. Article 18 of the Framework Act on Administrative Affairs lets an agency retroactively cancel an unlawful or improper decision, and where the decision was obtained by deception or other wrongful means, it need not weigh the harm to the person against the public interest first. So the missing word in the tax accountant law does not mean the pass cannot be taken back.

04Freeze first, decide later

The finance ministry said on November 15 that it would move quickly, through the Certified Tax Accountant Qualification Review Committee, which sits under the National Tax Service, to suspend the effect of A's certificate and put the pass on hold, Yonhap News Agency reported.

Normally the ministry waits for an investigation to confirm the facts before punishing cheating. This time it said it would act fast because A had confessed. A hold is not a final decision. The penalties the law actually names, voiding the exam and the five-year ban, have not been announced.

05Does the next person in line get in?

"Top scorer" is not a legal status. The runner-up already passed, so for that person only the ranking changes.

The harder question is for whoever just missed the cut. Under Article 8 of the act's enforcement decree, the second round is passed by scoring at least 40 in every subject and averaging at least 60. If fewer people clear that bar than the minimum number of passes the government sets in advance, the remaining places go to the highest averages.

Say the minimum is 700 and only 500 people average 60 or more. The other 200 places are filled in score order. The question only arises in a year when the minimum had to be topped up this way; if enough people cleared 60 on their own, nobody below the line is affected. If one of those results is voided, 699 people have passed. The decree has no clause for handing the empty place to the next candidate, and the government has not said whether it will.

06The accounting exam answers to a different law

A also said the same method was used on the first round of the certified public accountant exam, according to several Korean outlets. That exam falls under the CPA Act, and cheating there is handled by the Financial Services Commission. That law requires a cheat's pass to be revoked, followed by a five-year ban.

Passing the tax exam does not by itself let anyone practice. Under Article 6 of the tax accountant act, a new accountant must be entered in the finance ministry's register before taking on clients.

07What to watch

First, which charges the police pursue, and whether the investigation turns up cheating by other candidates. Second, when the National Tax Service decides to void A's exam and impose the ban.

Two questions now sit with the government and the National Assembly: whether to give the tax accountant law a power to revoke a pass, along with rules for the place it leaves empty, and how to keep electronic devices out of exam halls.
