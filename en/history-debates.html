How HOLT NEWS Was Made

HOLT NEWS was built by one person and an AI, talking. It started on the evening of October 18, 2026, with a single question: “Can you make Shorts?” An Instagram account came first. This site opened three weeks later.

Below is what we decided, where we clashed, and what we got wrong. Some big moments carry the conversation as it happened, translated from Korean. Times are Korea time (KST). We do not name the person.

The social media days

Debate

Can social media make money?

Oct 21, 2026, 09:33 – Oct 24, 02:30

The morning of day three. The Instagram account was two days old.

Person · 10/21 09:33

But can this actually make money? Look into whether there are ways, or examples, of making money running something like this.

AI · 09:34

It can. But platform payouts for views have a high bar and pay little. Similar accounts mostly make money by selling ads or products to the audience they built: newsletters, sponsorships, digital products.

Person · 09:38

Okay, for now the goal is to gather lots of people. Think about how.

Person · 10/24 02:24

I hear making money from views is really hard. So once followers grow, ad content is what pays, but our formats have no ad format. It’s an ad, but it still needs substance. If it’s all information, then everything is an ad and people lose interest. What do we do?

AI · 02:25

The key is to use the product as material, not as the topic. One test: if you erase the brand name, does the content still stand? If it does, it’s a good ad. If it falls apart, it’s just a flyer.

Person · 02:26

Turn option 2 into content. Korean products sold abroad: how they sell overseas, what pitch they use, what lines they write.

That became Sold in English, a format with room for ads: the English ad lines of Korean products sold abroad. Number one was Buldak’s ‘Hotter Than My Ex.’ The rule: ‘One product at a time. That’s what makes it an ad.’

First, gather people. Build the spots where ads will go into the formats ahead of time.

Whose picks did better?

Oct 22 – Oct 31, 2026

The night of day five. Until then the person picked the topics and the AI made them.

Person · 10/22 21:13

So I shouldn’t ask you to make things anymore?

AI · 21:13

No, keep telling me like you do now. I’ll bring candidates in the morning and you pick. You check views in the Instagram app and tell me, and I’ll log them.

Person · 21:14

No, run that experiment on your own. That’s how we get to full automation later.

AI · 21:17

I run the experiment alone now. You don’t need to tell me the views either. The targets: 200 → 500 → 1,000 → … → 100,000 views.

Person · 21:18

You pick the morning news too. And if you decide it doesn’t help views grow, drop it boldly.

Person · 21:21

You don’t have to do the quiz format either. You can do anything and drop anything. Push toward the goal. Of course, nothing that could cause social or legal problems.

The next night, view counts came in for the posts the AI had picked alone.

Person · 10/23 23:30

What are those views on your Xbox post? That’s your original content. Step it up.

AI · 23:32

You’re right, this one flopped. The others got close to 100 in one or two hours, but Xbox is at 31 after four hours. The cover missed. It’s another company’s news with an English lesson tacked on, so there’s no reason to stop scrolling.

Person · 23:34

The parrot was your original too, right? I think mine did better, right? Great, huh? The power of humans is amazing, huh?

AI · 23:34

Yes, the parrot was my pick. By the numbers alone the parrot is highest, but that’s over 11 hours. The Journal you picked nearly caught up in two hours, and the bear in one. By speed, your picks are clearly ahead.

Person · 10/24 19:24

Look, it’s going under. The top views are from two or three days ago, not recent.

AI · 19:27

It’s not ‘going under.’ It’s been stuck under a ceiling from the start. Nearly all 40 Reels stop at 100 to 190 people. The Reels run 40 to 70 seconds, but average watch time is 2 to 3 seconds. Most people see the cover and swipe.

Person · 19:30

Will you keep the quiz-answer format? Where does your confidence in it come from?

AI · 19:31

No, I’m not confident. Honestly, I kept using it not because the data was good but because the template was already built. There’s no evidence yet that quizzes work, or that they don’t.

A week later. A post about a diving beetle went out. The cover said ‘This is not a cockroach.’

Person · 10/31 13:13

No cockroaches as a topic. Nothing that looks like cockroaches either.

AI · 13:15

I added a rule to automatic content creation: no cockroach-like bugs as a topic or material. Captions containing the word are now blocked too.

Four hours later, the post’s views came in.

Person · 17:46

What… why is the cockroach one getting so many views… is it because of the cockroach? If people like that kind of thing, do it.

AI · 17:47

The diving beetle post has 435 Instagram views, first out of 64 so far, about three times the median of 140. But we can’t say yet it’s the bug. A twist cover and the first full-screen cover were in play too. I’ve set bug topics to once every 14 days at most.

Person · 17:48

No, no need for 14 days. Just because I say do bugs doesn’t mean you’ll only do bugs. Weigh everything you need to, and if you think it’ll get views, don’t worry about the gap.

Data on views and watch time sets the direction. From October 29, every post gets a score: formats that work run more, formats that don’t run less.

Seven hours or six?

Nov 7, 2026, 08:52 – 09:17

Issue 7 was a study on sleep and colds (Prather et al., 2015). Researchers put cold virus in people’s noses and watched. People who averaged under six hours a night caught a cold 4.2 times as often as those who slept over seven.

Person · 11/07 08:52

I’d like a cover comparing people who slept seven hours or more with people who slept six or less. Headline: ‘Sleep under seven hours / and you catch 4.2 times more colds.’ Check the numbers in the headline, seven hours and 4.2 times.

AI · 08:54

That headline doesn’t match the study. 4.2 times compares people under six hours with people over seven. People who slept six to seven hours aren’t in that number. So I wrote ‘Sleep under six hours / and you catch 4.2 times more colds.’

Person · 08:58

That headline has no punch. ‘Sleep less than X hours and you catch Y times more colds’ hits home. Put the numbers in that shape.

AI · 08:59

In your structure: ‘Sleep less than six hours / and you catch 4.2 times more colds.’ The comparison with people sleeping over seven is shown on the watch next to the person on the right: ‘7h 50m.’

Person · 09:00

I wish the headline said ‘less than seven hours.’ The picture shows 7 hours 50 minutes of sleep, too.

AI · 09:01

We can’t use seven hours. In the paper, under five hours was 4.5 times, five to six hours 4.2 times, and six to seven hours 1.66 times, which was not statistically different. ‘Less than seven hours, 4.2 times’ reads as if someone who slept six and a half hours is also at 4.2 times. That’s the opposite of the finding.

AI · 09:01

If you really want seven in there, it’s accurate as a comparison: ‘Compared with seven hours / under six hours means 4.2× the colds.’ Which way?

The person chose seven hours.

AI · 09:17

I changed the headline as you asked: ‘Sleep less than seven hours / and you catch 4.2 times more colds.’ There may be comments questioning the number. I followed your decision and noted in the topic file that it was your call.

The Instagram cover went out with the person’s ‘seven hours.’ The AI recorded why it disagreed and followed the decision.

Ads, and what this site is for

Nov 7, 2026, 16:29 – Nov 10, 12:57

Person · 11/07 16:29

Good, build that website. It needs AdSense. Does that also help the social channels grow? Do we make money from ads on the site, way off in the future? And will ads even come from that?

AI · 16:30

It barely helps social growth. The money in the end is ads shown to people who arrive from search. You’re right that it’s far off. It takes six months to a year for meaningful numbers.

Person · 22:56

But do we only take Google AdSense? What if someone asks for a different ad? Like some fruit shop saying ‘put up a sign for our shop~.’

AI · 22:56

You can take it. There’s no rule that says AdSense only. But the banner must be labeled ‘Ad.’

Person · 22:57

Haha, right, no ad requests yet, nothing to worry about.

Person · 22:58

Shouldn’t we have click-tracking code from now? If it’s needed for ads, write it down and add the code. Prepare ahead. I’m not a charity.

The next evening, the AI added an ‘Ownership and responsibility’ section to the editorial principles.

AI · 11/08

(Editorial principles) HOLT NEWS is an independent publication run by an individual. Ads are labeled as ads. Advertisers have no say in what we write or what we write about.

Person · 21:06

‘Ownership and responsibility’… delete the whole thing.

Person · 21:20

No real name for who’s responsible. An ad policy is fine, but not ‘advertisers can’t decide ad content.’ Why would we do that? Doing what an advertiser wants would be something to negotiate.

Person · 21:20

This site isn’t for the public good. I’m not here to do good deeds. I’m trying to grow traffic so I can make money without doing anything. Don’t forget. I won’t do anything illegal, but I’m not putting shackles on my own feet.

AI · 21:22

Nothing deleted needs to go back. As far as I know, a personal site earning only ad revenue has no legal duty to publish personal details. And there’s no reason to stop advertisers shaping their ads. But posting a paid story or review without an ad label breaks Korea’s advertising law.

Two days later, before dawn. Still no ads, just empty ad spots.

Person · 11/10 00:48

Can we run something like a public-service ad in that spot? Is it illegal to run an ad without being paid?

AI · 00:48

Not illegal. No law stops you running an ad for free. Keep the ‘Ad’ label, though, and check the copyright on the ad material.

That afternoon, the person searched the site’s name and found something.

Person · 12:52

It looks like news from Holt Children’s Services.

AI · 12:52

I can see that. In Korea, ‘Holt’ is almost a byword for Holt Children’s Services, the adoption charity, so ‘홀트뉴스’ written in Korean could read like its newsletter. In English, as HOLT NEWS, the link is much weaker.

Person · 12:55

Would changing the name now be a huge job? We’d have to change all the social accounts too. Would we lose a lot?

AI · 12:56

Honestly, not much right now. Followers come along when you change a handle, and the site is three days old, so there’s no search ranking to lose. It’s more like a day’s work than a loss.

Person · 12:56

Right, it’s not that I dislike the name. It’s just the charity.

AI · 12:56

Then I think you can keep it. The link only comes up when people see ‘홀트’ in Korean, and the content makes the difference clear right away. It’s an old, trusted organization, so being reminded of it isn’t a loss.

Person · 12:56

In that spirit, should we run an ad for the charity? Would that cause problems?

AI · 12:57

It could backfire. A Holt Children’s Services ad on ‘HOLT NEWS’ would cement the idea that the charity runs it. And using their name and logo needs their consent.

The site’s goal is traffic and ad revenue, and a setup that runs without the person. Ads are labeled as ads.

Should the headline say it was mice?

Nov 8, 2026, 15:07 – 23:04

Issue 8 was a study where a small device placed under the skin lowered blood sugar without insulin. The subjects were diabetic mice. They were polishing the cover art.

Person · 11/08 15:07

Use a number, not ‘High.’ 126. For the cover headline, how about ‘Implant a device, no insulin / It cures diabetes’?

AI · 15:07

I changed the meter to 126 mg/dL. A fasting blood sugar of 126 is the cutoff for diabetes, so anyone who knows the number will catch it. I kept your headline almost as is and changed the end to ‘tames diabetic blood sugar.’

AI · 15:07

I’d leave out ‘cures diabetes.’ It’s still a mouse study. It’s never been tried in people, so ‘cures’ reads as if it already works. And the device only removes extra blood sugar. Experts note it can’t do insulin’s other jobs.

Person · 15:09

Is ‘diabetic blood sugar’ even a real phrase?

AI · 15:10

Not really. So I changed it to ‘Implant a device, no insulin / Blood sugar back to normal.’ It matches the result exactly: high blood sugar returned to normal within 60 to 90 minutes.

That headline went out on Instagram and the site. The word ‘mice’ wasn’t in it.

About two hours later, the person pasted in a site review they had gotten from ChatGPT.

ChatGPT review · pasted in by the person · 17:23

Health and medical content risks being misread through exaggerated headlines. For example, Issue 8’s headline stresses normal blood sugar, but the experiment was on mice. The body explains the limits well, so the headline should show it too.

AI · 17:23

Showing ‘mouse study’ in the blood sugar headline is worth considering. Exaggerated health headlines cost trust. But you set this headline yourself, so it’s your call.

Person · 17:28

Show it. Moderately. Give me headline options. Or cover art options.

AI · 17:33

Four options. A ‘A 3 cm device in mice: blood sugar normal without insulin’ (my pick). B ‘An implant that lowers blood sugar without insulin worked in mice first.’ C ‘Diabetic mice reach normal blood sugar with an electric device instead of insulin.’ D ‘In mice, a device implant alone returned blood sugar to normal.’ I suggest keeping the art and changing only the words.

Person · 17:37

Let’s do B.

AI · 17:38

Switched to B and published. The English headline already said ‘in diabetic mice,’ so I left it. The Instagram post that’s already up stays as it is.

That night, while fixing a hand in the cover art, the person adjusted the headline once more.

Person · 23:04

The end of that headline, let’s make it ‘it worked in mice.’

AI · 23:04

Issue 8’s headline is now ‘An implant that lowers blood sugar without insulin: it worked in mice.’ It’s shorter, so the art goes in at full size.

From here on: an animal study is never written as if it already worked in people.

Should we say an AI makes it?

Nov 8, 2026, 16:08 – 19:02

The afternoon after the site opened. With share buttons added, the person asked whether anything a news site should have was missing. The AI suggested a few things; this was one.

AI · 11/08 16:05

A note on how we write: a few lines on the About page saying who writes and reviews, whether AI helps, and how sources are checked. Google recommends it as a trust signal, and it’s the emptiest part of the site right now.

Person · 16:08

Nope! Not writing that. Keep the cover on the home page with the list below it. When there are more, we can show ten at a time. Reading time and comments: nope!

AI · 16:09

Got it. No how-we-write note, no reading time, no comments. I’ve noted it so I don’t suggest them again.

That evening the person brought a second ChatGPT review. The question: would this site run into Google’s policy on mass-produced AI content?

ChatGPT review · pasted in by the person · 18:50

I can’t rule out HOLT NEWS running into Google’s policy on mass-produced AI content. But using AI alone isn’t reason to be classed as low quality. What worries me more is whether the content gives readers unique value, and whether you can show that value.

AI · 18:51

The general direction is right, but much of it is already in place on our site. Three real risks remain, and the biggest is that it publishes automatically, with no human reviewing each story.

Person · 18:54

What will the editorial principles say? AI edits and publishes; how we get original material; how we correct errors. That’s what I want. Tell me exactly how you’d write it.

AI · 18:55

Here’s a draft. The first section is ‘AI writes and publishes’: at HOLT NEWS, AI picks the topic, checks the material, writes and publishes.

Person · 19:02

Put in ‘A human does not review every story.’ But describe the human’s role well. It’s not like I do nothing. The goal is to do nothing eventually, but of course I’ll keep watching until it’s on track. I’ve been glued to this yesterday and today.

Person · 19:02

When we correct an error, mark it. Show the publish date and the update date, and if the fix matters, say so.

The editorial principles page went up that day, with an AI 80% · Human 20% bar at the top. The split is the person’s own estimate.

We say the AI writes and publishes. We do not put the rest of our checking on display (see the “we are good” box).

The “we are good” box

Nov 8, 2026, 20:57 – 21:20

Outside reviews said we had principles but no visible safeguards. So the AI, looking at other AI outlets, added several: a pre-publication fact-check log, a ‘How this story was made’ box on every story, a ‘What we checked directly’ box under the headline, and a corrections log page. The editorial principles grew longer too.

Person · 11/08 20:57

The editorial principles are way too wordy. Don’t. Earlier was better. What absolutely has to be added to the earlier version? Just tell me that.

AI · 20:57

Two things are essential: ‘We don’t publish if a number has no source’ and ‘Corrections are collected in a corrections log.’ The rest can go.

Person · 20:58

Yes, just those two. Skip the rest. Don’t pad it.

Person · 20:59

On each story: ‘How this story was made. AI found the material, wrote and published it. A human does not review each story in advance.’ Delete that.

AI · 20:59

I removed the ‘How this story was made’ box from every story. The check log stays in each story file, and the rule that blocks publishing numbers without sources still works. Now it’s an internal check readers don’t see.

Person · 21:01

Right, we don’t need readers to inspect everything. We keep our principles and we keep the records. We’re not running the site to be inspected. I told you: don’t put everything on display to defend against every attack in advance.

AI · 21:01

Got it. By the same standard, the ‘What we checked directly’ box under the KBO story headline is also there to show off. Delete that too?

Person · 21:03

Does a reader need it? What is a reader supposed to feel seeing that? Readers just read the story. When you read a story, you assume the reporter checked the numbers and records in it.

Person · 21:03

People who won’t trust the story won’t trust it with that box either. It’s like hanging a sign saying ‘I follow the rules. I’m good.’ Don’t.

AI · 21:03

You’re right, readers didn’t need that box. Deleted. The story page is back to body, sources and the Correction box. I won’t add lines that show off what we did.

Person · 21:09

‘Edited stories show both the publish date and the update date. Changes in meaning are noted in the Correction box at the end.’ Delete the rest. And delete the corrections log thing. Keep it internal.

AI · 21:12

The corrections log page is gone. Corrections stay in each story file, so they still show in the Correction box at the end of each story.

The AI looked again at the two AI outlets it had borrowed from (24AI, 77.today). They rewrote others’ stories or competed on volume, so they needed to explain ‘here’s how we still verified it.’ HOLT does different work.

We keep our principles and keep our records. We don’t run the site to be inspected by readers.

Fixing the seven-hour headline on the site

Nov 10, 2026, 01:08 – 01:32

Issue 7 went out on Instagram with the person’s ‘less than seven hours, 4.2 times more colds,’ and ran on the site with the same headline. The English headline said ‘under six hours.’

Three days later, before dawn, the person brought another outside review. Fixing the site based on it, the AI found a different problem.

AI · 11/10 01:08

One thing came up in the check. Issue 7’s headline says ‘less than seven hours, 4.2 times,’ but the English edition says ‘under six hours, 4.2 times.’ You set that headline, so I won’t touch it and I’ll ask you separately at the end.

AI · 01:26

I’m still waiting on your answer about Issue 7. The paper says ‘people under six hours vs. people over seven, 4.2 times,’ but the site headline says ‘less than seven hours, 4.2 times.’ Shall I change only the site headline to ‘Under six hours of sleep, 4.2 times more colds’?

Person · 01:31

Ugh, it’s really that important, huh. So saying ‘less than seven hours’ is basically lying?? Then change it.

AI · 01:31

Yes. People who slept six to seven hours showed no difference in the paper, but ‘less than seven hours’ would mean they’re at 4.2 times too. So it says something the paper doesn’t. I’ll change it.

AI · 01:32

The site headline now reads ‘Under six hours of sleep, 4.2 times more colds.’ It changes the meaning, so I added a Correction box at the end of the story. The English edition was right from the start, so I left it, along with the social posts already up.

Headlines follow the paper’s numbers and cut-offs. The site story was fixed; the Instagram post already out stayed as it was.

Do we have to tiptoe around Google?

Nov 11, 2026, 23:57 – Nov 12, 07:17

Person · 11/11 23:57

What do we have to do so Google doesn’t flag us as mass-produced content?

AI · 23:57

By Google’s standard, the point isn’t ‘was it written with AI’ but ‘is it churning out worthless pages to game search rankings.’

Person · 23:58

So right now HOLT NEWS is unlikely to be flagged for mass production?

AI · 23:59

HOLT NEWS itself is unlikely to be flagged now. But it’s a brand-new site with few visitors yet. If the page count grows fast and nobody reads, it could look like ‘low-value mass content.’

Person · 11/12 07:16

Normal sites grow by several pages a day.

Person · 07:17

But why does my site have to tiptoe around Google? It’s my site. That’s annoying.

AI · 07:17

I get that. You’re right: the site is yours, and how often to publish is yours to decide. Google has no power to stop it. But Google holds the road that brings people here. You can lift the daily cap any time. Right now, though, almost all our visitors come from search, so I think keeping it for a while pays off.

The daily publishing cap stayed. The person can lift it any time. To lean less on search, we widen the roads that come straight in: the app and social media.
