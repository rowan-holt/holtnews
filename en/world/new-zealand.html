    Political landscape
    The British monarch is head of state and a governor-general stands in for the Crown, but politics is run by a single-chamber parliament and the prime minister. Since 1996 voters have cast one vote for a local member and one for a party, with seats shared out by party vote, and only once, in 2020, has a single party won a majority on its own. So elections are followed by coalition talks, and small parties carry real weight. After the 2023 election the National Party formed a government with ACT and New Zealand First, and in the November 2026 election the two big parties each took only about 30%, again leaving smaller parties holding the key. Parliament has Māori seats elected by Māori voters on a separate roll. How the 1840 Treaty of Waitangi between the British Crown and Māori chiefs should apply in law today is a major issue, and a bill to redefine the treaty's principles was voted down in April 2025 after large protests.

    Exports from pasture

    Grass grows all year, so cattle and sheep are raised outdoors and milk and meat are cheap to produce. Fonterra, a cooperative owned by dairy farmers, collects most of the milk and sells it as milk powder, butter and cheese, and China is its biggest customer. In 2026 Fonterra sold its consumer brands business to France's Lactalis to focus on ingredients for food companies. Kiwifruit exports go through Zespri.

    Tourism and education

    Tourists who come for the scenery and international students looking for an English-speaking education are the next-largest sources of foreign income after farm goods.

    Technology and energy

    Most electricity comes from hydro, geothermal and wind. A few companies lead world markets in narrow fields, such as accounting software maker Xero and Fisher & Paykel Healthcare, which makes respiratory care devices. Rocket Lab, which began in New Zealand, launches small rockets from the Māhia Peninsula on the North Island.

    Weaknesses

    It is far from big markets and has a small population, which makes it hard for manufacturing to grow. Exports are concentrated in dairy and in China, so the economy moves with milk prices and Chinese demand, and many people leave for higher wages in Australia.

    Place in the world

    A 1987 law banned nuclear weapons and nuclear-powered ships, and the United States suspended its defense commitment to New Zealand under their alliance treaty as a result. Even so, it is one of the Five Eyes, sharing intelligence with the United States, Britain, Canada and Australia. The Cook Islands and Niue are self-governing states in free association with New Zealand, so their people hold New Zealand citizenship, and Tokelau is a New Zealand territory. In 2025 the Cook Islands signed agreements with China without consulting Wellington first, and New Zealand paused aid payments, a plain sign of the contest over the Pacific islands. China is its largest export market. Balancing security ties with the West against trade that leans on China is the standing problem of its foreign policy.

    Relations with neighbors

    Australia's closest ally. It has kept a nuclear-free policy that bars nuclear-powered or nuclear-armed ships from its ports, and is deeply connected with the Pacific island states.

    Relations with Korea

    The two countries established diplomatic relations in 1962. About 6,000 New Zealanders served in the Korean War and 45 died. A free trade agreement took effect in 2015, and with it the yearly working holiday quota for young South Koreans rose to 3,000. At the APEC summit in Gyeongju in October 2025 the two countries upgraded their relationship to a comprehensive strategic partnership and agreed to keep discussing an update of the trade agreement. South Korea is New Zealand's fifth-largest trading partner.

    Things you may not know

    New Zealand was the first country to give women the vote in national elections, in 1893.
