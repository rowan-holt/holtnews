AI writes and publishes

At HOLT NEWS, AI picks the topics, checks the sources, writes the stories and publishes them. A person does not review each story.

People set the standards. We decide which news to cover, which sources to trust, what must never be left out and what tone to use. When we read a published story and find something lacking, we do not stop at fixing that one story. We fix the standard, so the same mistake does not happen in the next one.

For now, a person reads the stories every day and refines the standards. Our goal is to need less human work as the standards grow solid. Even then, people will set and fix the standards.

Before publishing, we compare each story with other reports on the same news. If it cannot add something those reports lack, it does not write the story. On days with nothing worth writing, we skip.

We go to original sources

We check facts and numbers where they first appeared: government and agency releases, original statistics, bills, official websites and research papers. The Sources box under each story lists only these original sources.

Anything not in the original sources is used only if several outlets reported it. We do not use reports that appeared in only one outlet. When original sources and news reports give different numbers, we follow the original sources.

For research, we check the methods, scale, numbers and limits in the paper itself. We also look at how it differs from earlier work and whether other studies found different results.

From first idea to later fixes

Every news story goes through ten steps. The standard and the work at each step are drawn in the diagrams below. Research pieces and the ECON 101 and LABOR 101 series choose their topics and are written a little differently, so they have their own diagrams.

1. Finding candidates. For each slot, we scan government and agency releases, statistics, company filings and news outlets at home and abroad, and gather more than ten topics.

2. Weighing candidates. Topics that cannot be checked against original sources, or that would only repeat earlier reports, are dropped. The rest get six scores of up to 5 points each, 30 in all. For news, they include importance, what we can add, search demand and community reaction; for research, study strength takes the place of community reaction.

3. Choosing. We write the candidate with the highest total among those scoring above 18. A field used in the last two stories loses 3 points, and scores within 1 point count as a tie, broken in favor of the story that cannot wait. Other candidates above the bar carry over to the next slot and are scored again with new ones. If none clears the bar, we keep looking.

4. Writing. The story is written from the original sources, and every number in it is tied to its source. We aim for full substance in short sentences, one point to a sentence, and explain unfamiliar systems and terms before they are needed so no gap leaves the reader wondering. We avoid hype and promotional language. The English edition is not a translation of the Korean one. It is written separately so that readers abroad can read it naturally and fully on its own. Real photos must show the actual event, place or subject, and before using one we check its source and terms of use (license and copyright) where it was published. If the terms are unclear, we do not use it.

5. Pre-publication review. If even one number has no source, the story is not published. A review kept separate from the AI that wrote the story checks the sources, conclusion, headline, what the story adds and the images again, and the English edition is reviewed on its own. A story that fails is fixed and reviewed again, or dropped.

6. Publishing. The files pass a safety check before they go live.

7. Telegram report. Each time we publish, a person receives the headline, time, the scores of the chosen topic and the other candidates, and the review results. When a slot is skipped, the reason is sent.

8. First check after publishing. Right after publishing, we open the live page and check that the headline, numbers and images came out correctly.

9. Regular checks. Every Friday, we pick at least 3 and at most 12 of the stories published so far and check them again, starting from the original sources. A story that has been checked comes back into the pool after four weeks, because we may have missed something the first time, or the sources may have changed since.

10. Outside feedback. Reader reports and outside reviews are checked against the original sources. If something is wrong, we fix it, and we change the standards in the earlier steps so the same mistake does not recur.

We do not list what was checked for each story. We keep those records ourselves, and when we find an error, we fix it and say so, as set out below.

We fix errors and say so

We check error reports every day. For news and research pieces alike, we verify each report against the original sources, and if the piece is wrong, we fix it within 48 hours.

A corrected piece shows both its published date and its updated date. When a fix changes the meaning, such as a fact or a number, we note what we changed and when in a Corrections box at the end of the piece. On those pieces, tap the updated date to jump straight to the box.

In news stories, the small number after a sentence with a figure points to where that figure came from. Tap it to jump to the same number in the sources at the end. Figures HOLT NEWS calculated itself carry no number.

Send reports through "Report an error" in each piece's ⋯ menu or through the contacts below.
